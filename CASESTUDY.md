Veggio

Veggio (vego, Australian slang for vegetarian, given an Italian inflection: the kind of word that sounds like it should mean "I eat vegetables.")
Veggio helps you eat more vegetables by making it easier to think of something worth cooking.
Live at veggio.lovable.app · Read the full case study

What it does
Tell Veggio what is in your fridge, describe a cuisine or mood, or hit Surprise me. Every time, you get three recipe options from different cuisines and cooking methods, each with a full ingredient list, step by step instructions and a nutritional estimate per serving. If none of the three land, generate again.
Log in to save favourites, confirm what you actually cooked, and complete a short onboarding flow that personalises every generation to your goals, your kitchen confidence and the ingredients you never want to see on your plate.

The three modes
What's in my fridge — list what you have and Veggio builds around it.
What I'm in the mood for — describe a cuisine, a vibe or a craving. Something Asian, quick and comforting, spicy, Mediterranean.
Surprise me — no input needed. Three diverse dishes from three different cuisines and cooking methods, every time.

Onboarding and personalisation
First-time logged-in users are walked through five questions: current vegetable intake, motivation, goal, ingredients to always avoid, and cooking confidence. That data shapes every generation. A user who wants quick and simple gets different recipes than one who is happy to spend time on something more involved.
Onboarding is optional and can be skipped or revisited at any time.

Cooking confirmation
Saving a recipe is curiosity. Cooking it is the real signal. Veggio asks "Did you make this?" when a recipe is expanded and again on saved recipes that have not yet been confirmed. That data will power nutritional analytics in a future version.

What was cut and why
Supplements, calorie targets and macro tracking were cut because they require clinical expertise the product cannot claim. Meal planning and shopping lists were descoped because they belong in a later version once the single-session experience is validated. Salads were considered and rejected: they are a different product with a different user. The full reasoning is in the case study.
